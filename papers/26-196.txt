1. Background
==============

26-150r3 rewrote 16.5.4.4 to respond to UTI025, and in doing so made
instantiation association of a deferred procedure considerably more
flexible. Unfortunately it left open a problem. If the hypothetical
deferred procedure as wrapper is taken literally, it would imply
that extra defined assignment or final subroutines would be invoked.
This is not intended.


2. Design
=========

The fix keeps the hypothetical reference - it is still the simplest
way to state the argument-correspondence requirements (INTENT, and,
via ordinary argument-association rules, the trailing-optional and
assumed-rank flexibility that NOTE 2 describes) and to say what
generic resolution is performed against - but says up front what it is
for: determining argument correspondence and, where applicable,
performing generic resolution. This makes it clearer that a reference
to the deferred procedure is simply a reference to the procedure
identified as its instantiation argument, with the same actual
arguments, and this does not entail any additional procedure
reference, intermediate result object, defined assignment, or
invocation of a final subroutine beyond what that direct reference
entails. We had also missed some rules about PURE and SIMPLE which
this new paragraph addresses.


3. Edits
========

    "The following is determined by considering a hypothetical
     procedure reference to an instantiation argument corresponding
     to a deferred procedure where the actual arguments provided are
     the dummy arguments of the deferred procedure in order.
     If the instantiation argument is a generic-spec, such a
     reference performs generic resolution and the selected specific
     procedure is the deferred procedure's corresponding procedure,
     otherwise the reference shall be valid and the instantiation
     argument is the deferred procedure's corresponding procedure.
     If the INTENT of a dummy argument of the deferred procedure is:
     * IN, the corresponding dummy argument of the corresponding
       procedure shall be INTENT(IN);
     * INOUT, the corresponding dummy argument of the corresponding
       procedure shall be INTENT(INOUT) or INTENT(OUT);
     * OUT, the corresponding dummy argument of the corresponding
       procedure shall be INTENT(OUT);
     * unspecified, the corresponding dummy argument of the
       corresponding procedure may have any INTENT.
     If the deferred procedure is PURE, the corresponding procedure
     shall be PURE. If the deferred procedure is SIMPLE, the
     corresponding procedure shall be SIMPLE. Upon instantiation,
     references to the deferred procedure are references to the
     corresponding procedure."
